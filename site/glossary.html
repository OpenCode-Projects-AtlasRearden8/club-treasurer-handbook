Glossary — Plain-English Money Words

Every term a club treasurer needs, explained like a friend would. Members are welcome to read this too — that's the point.

2FA (two-factor authentication) A second proof of "it's really you" at login — usually a code from your phone. Turn it on everywhere you can, especially the bank.

Advance Money paid early against a future bill, like a venue deposit. Recorded as "already promised," then booked as the real expense when the thing happens.

Approval (two-eyeballs rule) A second, different person says yes to a payout before it happens. The spender is never their own approver. Cheapest anti-fraud tool ever invented.

Backup The reserve account. Old word, new use — see Chapter 2.

Balance How much money is in an account right now. The first line of every monthly report because it's the one everybody reads.

Bank signatory (signer) A person the bank authorizes to do things on the club's account. Keep at least two per account, and remove anyone whose job ended.

Bounce A payment that fails because there isn't enough money in the account. A whole chapter of anxiety you avoid by keeping an operating cushion.

Budget The plan for the year's money, per category. Not required to run this handbook's system, but handy: the monthly report's "budget bars" compare plan-vs-actual.

Category A short label for what money was for — dues, events, supplies, venue, food, equipment, misc. Four to eight of them. Longer lists are where ledger sanity goes to die.

Close-out (year-end close) The December-ish ritual: reconcile everything, write the final report, build the handoff binder, and hand the keys over cleanly.

Deposit (money in) Money added to the bank account — dues, event income, donations. Also, confusingly, an "advance" paid to a vendor (see Advance). Context is telling.

Discrepancy When two numbers that should match, don't. Normal, findable, fixable — the monthly reconcile exists exactly to catch these while they're small.

Float The fixed starting amount in a petty cash box (say $40). If you can't remember why the box has $40, that's the float.

Handoff binder The labeled binder (or folder) you hand your successor: start-here page, accounts, ledger, reports, receipts, vendors, dues, and contacts. The most generous object in club treasurership.

Hardship policy A club rule letting dues be waived or reduced for members going through a rough patch. If the club has none, suggest one to the officers — kindly.

Invoice The vendor's bill: date, who, what, how much, how to pay. Match it before paying; file it after.

Ledger The book of every dollar in and every dollar out, one line per transaction, with date, description, category, account, and receipt status. The single page of truth for the whole job.

Monthly report The one-pager members actually read: balances, money in, money out, transfers, one-line health note. Posted every month, even boring months — because it's boring that they trust you.

Net income Money in minus money out for a period. Positive is fine, negative is fine once (that's what operating cushion and reserves are for), chronically negative twice in a row is a conversation with the officers.

Offline books Ledger-without-cloud — any notebook or local spreadsheet that follows this handbook's ledger rules. Totally valid.

Peer approval See Approval. It's important enough to appear twice.

Petty cash A small cash box for tiny meeting purchases. Legitimate only if counted monthly, receipted at payout, and given a fixed float. If you're tempted to skip any of those, close the box.

Purse / Treasury Old words for "the money," found in older club bylaws. Same job as described in this handbook.

Receipt Proof a purchase happened: date, vendor, amount, description. Required before any reimbursement is paid, from anyone, ever. The kindest rule in the book.

Reconcile Comparing your ledger to the bank statement, line by line, so they agree. Monthly, 30 minutes, catches everything while it's still small.

Reimbursement Paying a member back for club-approved spending from their own pocket. Needs the form, the receipt, and an approver who isn't the spender.

Reserve The second of the two accounts — savings for later, touched rarely and on purpose, no debit card. See Chapter 2.

Reconciling item A transaction that landed in one place but not yet the other (a check not cashed; a deposit posted late). Not an error — just note it and watch for it next month.

Standing quote A documented rate agreed with a vendor for recurring work (venue rental, monthly snacks). Re-quote once a year, then ride it.

Two-eyeballs rule See Approval. Yes. Twice. It's the most important habit in the book.

Vendor Anyone the club pays for goods or services, from venues to snack suppliers to the person who makes the banner. Every vendor gets a log line, and every vendor gets fair, documented pay.

W-9 / 1099 territory Tax paperwork that can attach to payments to individuals in some places. This handbook doesn't go there — that's a question for a tax professional (a CPA or enrolled agent), asked before the payment season, not during it.
