# The Club Treasurer Handbook

*A friendly, education-only manual for the volunteer treasurer everybody depends on.*

Welcome. If you've just been handed a box of receipts and a bank login, take a
breath: you can do this. This handbook walks you through the whole job in plain
English — taking over without dread, keeping a simple two-account setup,
building receipt and reimbursement habits you won't resent, writing monthly
reports people actually read, paying vendors fairly (with the paperwork to
prove it), collecting dues kindly, assembling a year-end handoff binder, and
basic fraud-proofing. By the end, you'll be running a small, boring, healthy
money operation — and "boring" is the goal.

## Who this is for

Volunteer treasurers of small clubs and community groups: game clubs, hobby
associations, PTO-style support groups, maker groups, neighborhood stacks. You
are not an accountant. You don't need to be. You need consistent habits, a
short list of documents, and the confidence to ask questions out loud.

## What this is — and what it is not

This handbook is **education only**. It is **never legal advice and never tax
advice**. Every club, state, and
organization is different, and rules about money and tax status are exactly the
kind of thing where getting generic guidance wrong gets expensive.

**If your question involves 501(c) status, tax filings, insurance, or your
club's legal structure — stop and ask a professional.** That means a tax
professional (CPA or enrolled agent) for tax questions, a lawyer for legal
ones, or your club's board of directors for internal policy. There's even a
twice-a-year cliff-notes version of the question: "Is there anything here
we're required to file?" — a great thing to bring to a pro, and not a fun
thing to guess.

## How to use this book

1. Start with [Taking Over](01-taking-over.html) — it gets you from "oh no" to
   "OK, I've got this" in one sitting.
2. Set up your accounts with [The Two-Account Setup](02-two-accounts.html).
3. Skim one habit chapter a week: [Receipts & Reimbursements](03-receipts-and-reimbursements.html),
   [Monthly Reports](04-monthly-reports.html), [Paying Vendors](05-paying-vendors.html),
   [Collecting Dues](06-collecting-dues.html).
4. Use the templates in [`templates/`](../templates/) as your starter kit — a
   ledger sheet, a monthly report template, a reimbursement form, and more.
5. When your term ends (or when you're planning ahead), follow
   [The Year-End Handoff](07-year-end-handoff.html) and
   [Fraud-Proofing Basics](08-fraud-proofing.html).

Everything here works with a paper binder, a spreadsheet, or anything in
between. Nothing in this book assumes you buy software.

## The one-paragraph version of the whole job

Keep two bank accounts (operating and reserve), record every dollar in and out
in one ledger, reimburse people only with a receipt and one signature from
someone other than the spender, publish a one-page money report every month,
pay vendors at documented fair rates with a paper trail, chase dues gently and
consistently, hand your successor a labeled binder, and make stealing boringly
difficult instead of thrillingly easy. That's the book. The chapters just
unpack it.
