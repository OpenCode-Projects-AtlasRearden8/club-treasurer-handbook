# Chapter 6 — Collecting Dues Kindly

*Dues are how the club happens. Kind and consistent beats dramatic every time.*

Nothing about money makes a volunteer more anxious than asking fellow members
for money — and nothing about the job matters quite so much. The good news:
kind dues collection is a *system*, not a personality trait. Run the same
three reminders at the same time every cycle, and the drama disappears.

## The system: one reminder rhythm

Pick a dues cycle (monthly, quarterly, yearly — whatever the club set) and
announce one fixed rhythm out loud, once:

1. **Due date announced** at the meeting, in the report, and wherever the
   club posts things. People can't pay a deadline they don't know.
2. **Gentle reminder at the due date.** "Dues for [month/quarter] are due
   today — thanks to everyone who's in! Here's [payment link/instructions]."
   That's the whole message. No shame, no list of names.
3. **Two friendly nudges,** a week apart, if needed — private (to the
   person), not a public naming. Then **list the person as "in progress"**
   and stop chasing: some months people are broke, busy, or both. Consistent
   privacy is what makes this kind.

Private... patient... consistent... and always with the door open. That's it.

## The dues tracker (one row per member, one column per cycle)

[The template](../templates/dues-tracker.md) has one row per member and a
column per cycle. It answers the only three questions that matter: who's paid,
who isn't yet, and how much is expected. Update it as payments arrive; bring
the totals (never the names) into your monthly report.

If the club wants a paid-member list, keep it *current* and group it (e.g.,
"34 of 40 paid") in public. Naming unpaid members in a club-wide message is
the fastest way to convert "I'm having a rough month" into "I'm leaving the
club."

## What kindness looks like in practice

- **Default to the assumption of an oversight,** never a scandal. "Hey, I
  bet this just slipped — dues for April are past due whenever you get a
  chance" is the tone of the whole job.
- **Offer payment options people actually have.** At minimum: cash at the
  meeting (get a receipt for THEM — see below), a check, and the least-clunky
  electronic option the club's bank supports. Not everyone has Venmo; not
  everyone has a checkbook.
- **Never collect cash alone.** Two people count it, both initial the ledger,
  same day. This protects the payer and protects *you* — the two-person rule
  is worth the half-minute of logistics every single time.
- **Privately offer a real path for a member who genuinely can't pay this
  cycle:** "The club has a policy option — you can talk to the president about
  a waived or reduced rate. Want me to connect you?"   You are not the policy;
  you're the person who knows where the door is. If the club has no hardship
  policy, suggest one *to the officers*, not to the member in front of them.

## The receipt you give back

**Every dues payment gets a receipt to the payer,** even cash, even small.
A $10 receipt with the date, amount, and "dues — March" is 20 seconds of
writing (or an auto-email from your app), and it means the member always has
proof — which is what makes "did I pay?" arguments impossible later. Keep the
other copy in your files like anything else.

## When members don't pay (the calm decision tree)

1. **Reminders ran, private nudges done.** Mark them unpaid that cycle. Move
   your attention to the club's actual work.
2. **Two cycles unpaid:** a kind private check-in — not collecting, just
   "everything OK?" Half the time they'll pay on the spot; the other half
   you'll learn something true and useful.
3. **Persistent non-payment is a membership-policy question, not a
   treasurer question.** Take it to the officers with a name and a total —
   once, gently — then let the club's policy handle it. The treasurer's job
   is facts + kindness; the *policy* belongs to the club.

## Dues money hygiene

- Record dues in the ledger on arrival, by source (member count × amount, one
  line per collection event is fine).
- Bank it promptly. A "dues envelope" sitting in a binder all season is a
  fraud-risk and an embarrassing-loss risk. Deposit on the same day-ish.
- Dues money goes in Operating — never Reserve, never an "envelope for later"
  — never a "we'll sort it out at year-end" pile. It's this year's money.
