# Chapter 4 — Monthly Reports Members Actually Read

*One page. Plain words. Every month. Even boring months.*

A monthly report is not an accounting duty. It is how a club becomes the kind
of club where money is boring — which is the kind where nobody worries, nobody
suspects, and nobody has to be talked into being treasurer next year.

The template (`templates/monthly-report-template.md`) is one page. Fill it in
fifteen minutes and post it where the club already talks — email, chat, the
bulletin board, read-aloud at the meeting. All four are fine. Consistency is
the only requirement.

## The anatomy of a one-pager

1. **The month and the balances.** Operating and Reserve, start and end.
   Two lines. This is 80% of what people want to know.
2. **Money in this month** — one line per source: "Dues $180, event door $92,
   donations $25." No categories nobody uses. No suspense.
3. **Money out this month** — one line per meaningful purchase. You may group
   small food purchases; you may NOT group "various $173." If someone could
   ask "wait, what was that?" you've grouped wrong.
4. **Transfers** — Operating/Reserve moves on the record, with the reason.
5. **The one-line health note.** One sentence, human-voice:
   "We're fine. Snacks are slightly over budget because we hosted twice.
   Dues are at 70% collected."
6. **Coming months (optional but magic).** "September will include the $250
   speaker fee and pickup of the banner." Sets expectations, kills surprises.

## Writing it so people read it

- **Plain sentences.** Not `OP ACCT MTM VAR +4.2%` — "Operating gained $84
  this month." If your report needs a decoder, it will not be read.
- **Round softly.** $238.17 can be $238. Whole dollars, everywhere, unless a
  penny matters (and at club scale it doesn't).
- **No jargon without a friend.** The glossary at the back of this handbook is
  written for members, not accountants. Steal its wording.
- **Same order, every month.** Members learn to find the number they care
  about in two seconds. Novelty in format is friction; novelty in *content*
  is fine.
- **Show category budget bars if you like.** `Venue: $80 of $100 [======= ]`.
  One glance, zero math. Extra credit; not required.

## What "every month" means when the month is boring

Boring months get the shortest report of all:

> "March: no income, no spending. Operating $1,240, Reserve $500.
> Next month: game night (~$30 snacks)."

That's it. In fact, a short boring report is the *best* one — it says
"nothing is hiding" every single month, and it keeps you in the habit so a
weird month doesn't catch you silent.

## What the report does for *you*

- **It's your monthly self-audit's output.** Writing "money out this month"
  forces the Chapter 3 reconciliation. The report and the audit are one act.
- **It's your paper trail for the handoff.** Twelve reports = your year,
  documented, in a binder (Chapter 7).
- **It's your defense in weird months.** If spending spikes in October, the
  report of September already flagged it would. Surprises become conversations
  instead of suspicions.

## Handling questions (a gift, not an attack)

When someone asks "why was food $112?" — that is the system **working**.
Answer plainly at the meeting or in the thread: "Two hosted nights and the
pizza went up. I've switched to the grocery-store run, should drop to ~$70."
Then move on. Nobody needs a hearing. A treasurer who answers the first
question visibly never encounters the fifteenth.

If a question stumps you: "Good catch — let me check the receipt and I'll
correct or explain at the next report." Then actually do that. Correcting
your own ledger in public is the strongest trust move in the entire
professional sport of volunteering.

## The rhythm (put it in your calendar once, keep it forever)

1. **Day 1-ish:** pull bank statements for last month.
2. **Same sitting:** reconcile (Chapter 3's four checks) — 30 minutes.
3. **Same sitting:** fill the one-pager from your reconciled ledger — 15.
4. **Post it** the same day, same channel, every month.
5. **Read-aloud version** at the meeting: 60 seconds, the health note plus
   anything people will react to.

Total: about 45–60 minutes a month. That is the entire reporting commitment
in exchange for a club that trusts its money completely.

## Report anti-patterns (learn from others' pain)

- **The annual mega-report.** Twelve monthly one-pagers beat one 40-slide
  deck in December. Nobody reads the deck; everybody reads twelve friendly
  pages.
- **The defensive report.** Long pre-emptive explanations of how busy you've
  been, buried spending, footnotes reading like a hostage statement. If the
  numbers are clean, say them plainly. If they're muddy, fix the muddiness —
  that's a ledger problem from Chapter 3, not a words problem.
- **The vanishing treasurer.** No report the month after an event is how
  rumors start. Especially when the month is complicated — post *something*:
  "Event income is still arriving at the bank; full report next month; here's
  what's posted so far: [list]." Silence costs more than partial info.
